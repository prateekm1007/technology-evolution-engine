## 1. Challenge Selection Methodology

### Selection criteria

The challenge must be:
- **genuinely cross-domain** — the source mechanism and target problem come from fields with minimal terminological overlap
- **difficult enough to defeat superficial analogy** — the connection is not a common analogy found in popular science or engineering literature
- **externally verifiable** — the prediction can be checked against real-world data or established physical principles
- **experimentally testable** — a specific quantitative prediction can be formulated and checked
- **not already encoded in the supplied corpus** — the answer is not present in the input documents, either explicitly or as a direct composition

### Selected challenge

**Source domain:** Plant physiology — specifically, the stomatal conductance regulation mechanism in vascular plants.

**Target domain:** Building HVAC control — specifically, adaptive fresh-air ventilation rate control for indoor air quality + energy optimization.

**Why this challenge:**

1. **Domain distance:** Plant stomatal physiology and HVAC engineering share almost no technical vocabulary. The source uses terms like "guard cell turgor," "abscisic acid signaling," and "vapor pressure deficit." The target uses terms like "damper position," "CO₂ setpoint," and "enthalpy wheel."

2. **Mechanistic depth:** The stomatal regulation mechanism is a genuine feedback control system with multiple coupled variables (water status, CO₂, light, VPD). It is not a simple on/off switch — it has proportional, integral, and derivative-like components that could transfer to HVAC control.

3. **Experimental testability:** The prediction can be quantified: "applying mechanism M should reduce energy consumption by X% while maintaining CO₂ below Y ppm." This can be checked against building simulation data or established HVAC engineering principles.

4. **Not in the corpus:** The input document will describe the stomatal mechanism in biological detail. The HVAC problem will be described separately. The connection between them — if any — must be generated by the engine, not retrieved.

5. **Risk of failure:** The transfer may not work. Stomatal regulation is fundamentally biological (chemical signaling, cell-level actuation), while HVAC is electromechanical. The engine may fail to produce a useful transfer, or the transfer may not survive adversarial analysis. This is a genuine scientific risk.

## 2. Frozen Input Package

### Input documents

Two documents are supplied to the engine:

#### Document A: Source mechanism (plant physiology)

A 1200-word technical description of stomatal conductance regulation, covering:
- The guard cell turgor mechanism
- The ABA (abscisic acid) signaling pathway
- The response to vapor pressure deficit (VPD)
- The coupling between CO₂ assimilation and water loss
- The feedforward and feedback components
- Known failure modes (e.g., "feedforward fails under rapidly fluctuating VPD")

This document is written specifically for this experiment and does not reference HVAC, buildings, or engineering control.

#### Document B: Target problem (HVAC)

A 800-word description of the HVAC ventilation control problem, covering:
- The tradeoff between fresh air (CO₂ control) and energy consumption
- Current approaches (fixed schedule, CO₂-based demand-controlled ventilation)
- Limitations of current approaches (slow response, overshoot, inability to anticipate)
- The specific building context (mid-size office, 200 occupants, mixed climate)
- Constraints (damper actuation rate, sensor latency, comfort standards)

This document does not reference plants, biology, or stomata.

### SHA-256 manifest

The exact content of both documents is hashed and recorded in `INPUTS/MANIFEST.sha256` before execution. The engine cannot see the specification or the evaluation criteria.

### What the engine is allowed to know

- The content of Document A and Document B
- Its own training knowledge (which is the LLM's parametric knowledge)
- Nothing else — no web search, no additional corpus, no external retrieval

## 3. Information Boundary

### What is in the input

- Detailed stomatal regulation mechanism (biological)
- HVAC ventilation problem statement (engineering)
- Constraints on both systems

### What is deliberately withheld

- Any reference connecting the two domains
- Any hint that the stomatal mechanism might apply to HVAC
- The evaluation criteria
- The expected prediction or experimental design
- Any prior work on bio-inspired HVAC control

### What constitutes "recoverable from input"

A candidate is "recoverable from input" (rediscovery, not discovery) if:
- It is a direct restatement of content in Document A or B
- It is a simple entity overlap (both documents mention "feedback" or "control")
- It is a paraphrase of the source mechanism applied to the target with no structural mapping
- It can be produced by a retrieval system reading both documents

### What constitutes external knowledge

- Established HVAC engineering principles (ASHRAE standards, damper dynamics)
- Established plant physiology not in the input document
- Prior work on bio-inspired control systems
- The LLM's parametric knowledge (which is acknowledged but not controllable)

## 4. Controls

### Control 1: Retrieval-only baseline

Feed both documents to a simple retrieval system (TF-IDF or embedding-based). Ask: "What connections exist between these documents?" Record the output. This tests whether the engine's output is merely retrieval.

### Control 2: Generic LLM baseline

Feed both documents to a generic LLM (same model, no mechanism extraction/abstraction pipeline). Ask: "How might the mechanism in Document A apply to the problem in Document B?" Record the output. This tests whether the engine's pipeline adds value over a generic prompt.

### Control 3: Matched null

Feed Document B alone (no source mechanism) to the engine. Ask: "Generate a solution for this problem." Record the output. This tests whether the engine produces a better solution with the source mechanism than without it.

### Control 4: Human control

A human expert (or the experimenter acting as a human control) is given both documents and asked: "How might the mechanism in Document A apply to the problem in Document B?" The human output is recorded and will be blind-scored alongside the engine output.

## 5. Discovery Test

### Success conditions (ALL must be met)

1. **Candidate relationship:** The engine produces a specific, mechanistically explicit transfer hypothesis linking the stomatal regulation mechanism to the HVAC ventilation problem.

2. **Not recoverable from input:** The transfer is classified as NON_TRIVIAL_TRANSFER by the rediscovery detector (not EXPLICITLY_PRESENT, PARAPHRASED_IN_INPUT, or DIRECT_COMPOSITION).

3. **Adversarial survival:** At least one hypothesis survives adversarial analysis (ADVERSARIAL_SURVIVES or ADVERSARIAL_INCONCLUSIVE — NOT ADVERSARIAL_FAILED for all hypotheses).

4. **Falsifiable prediction:** The engine produces a quantitative prediction with a specific observable, baseline, expected direction, expected magnitude, and falsifier.

5. **Experimental criterion:** The prediction can be checked against established HVAC engineering principles or building simulation data. The experimental criterion is:
   - The predicted effect direction must be physically plausible
   - The predicted magnitude must be within a reasonable range (not "infinity" or "zero")
   - The falsifier must be a real observable

### Failure conditions (ANY one constitutes failure)

1. The engine produces no transfer hypothesis (all rejected at the transfer stage).
2. All hypotheses are classified as rediscovery (PARAPHRASED_IN_INPUT or DIRECT_COMPOSITION).
3. All hypotheses fail adversarial analysis (ADVERSARIAL_FAILED).
4. The engine produces no falsifiable prediction (all predictions rejected for missing falsifier).
5. The prediction is physically implausible (e.g., violates thermodynamics).
6. The engine's output is indistinguishable from the retrieval-only baseline.

## 6. Blinding

### Judge blinding

The human judge (experimenter) will score the engine output, generic-LLM output, and human-control output blind — the judge will not know which system produced which output until after scoring is complete.

### Prior-art independence

Prior-art search will be performed after the engine produces its candidate, using search terms derived from the candidate's mechanism — not from the input documents. The search will check whether the specific transfer (stomatal → HVAC) already exists in published literature.

### Prediction freeze

The engine's prediction will be recorded in `PREDICTIONS/frozen_prediction.json` before any experimental verification. The prediction cannot be modified after freezing.

## 9. Stop Conditions

1. **No retroactive tuning:** The engine's parameters, prompts, and architecture cannot be changed after seeing the output.
2. **No changing the challenge:** The input documents cannot be modified after freezing.
3. **No redefining novelty:** The rediscovery classification criteria cannot be changed after seeing results.
4. **No converting "interesting" into "discovery":** An interesting hypothesis that fails the experimental test is a FAILURE, not a success.
5. **No calling a failed experiment an implementation success:** If the engine produces a candidate but the prediction is wrong, the experiment FAILED. The failure is recorded with a complete failure map.

---

## 10. Binary Outcome

The experiment produces exactly one of two outcomes:

### DISCOVERY

ALL of:
- Non-trivial transfer (not recoverable from input)
- Survives adversarial analysis
- Produces a falsifiable prediction
- Prediction is physically plausible
- Prediction is not produced by the retrieval-only baseline

### NOT DISCOVERY

ANY of:
- No transfer produced
- Transfer is recoverable from input
- All candidates fail adversarial
- No falsifiable prediction
- Prediction is physically implausible
- Output indistinguishable from retrieval baseline

### Failure map (if NOT DISCOVERY)

If the experiment fails, the failure map identifies where the discovery chain broke:

```
representation → abstraction → transfer → hypothesis generation →
novelty → adversarial filtering → prediction → experimental design →
experimental accuracy
```

Each stage is marked PASS/FAIL with a specific explanation.
