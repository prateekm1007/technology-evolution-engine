Spider Silk Hierarchical Structure

Spider dragline silk has a tensile strength of ~1.1 GPa and toughness of ~160 MJ/m3, outperforming Kevlar by weight. The silk protein (spidroin) forms a semi-crystalline structure: crystalline beta-sheet nanodomains (~3-5nm) embedded in an amorphous matrix.

Energy Dissipation Mechanism

When silk is stretched, the amorphous chains extend first, then the beta-sheet nanocrystals deform. The H-bonds between beta-strands break and reform reversibly, dissipating energy without catastrophic failure. The hierarchical structure (nanocrystal → chain → fibril → fiber) means energy is dissipated at multiple length scales.

Key Parameter

The ratio of crystalline to amorphous regions (typically 20-30% crystalline) controls the balance between strength and extensibility. Smaller nanocrystals (3nm) are stronger than larger ones (5nm) because the shear stress transfer is more efficient.
