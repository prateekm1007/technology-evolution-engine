Dolphin Blubber Acoustic Properties

Dolphin blubber is a viscoelastic composite: lipid-filled adipose tissue with collagen fiber networks. The blubber layer (2-5cm thick) serves as acoustic window for echolocation, but ALSO attenuates unwanted sound.

Mechanism

The viscoelastic loss modulus (tan delta) peaks at specific frequencies, absorbing sound energy through internal friction. The collagen fiber network creates impedance gradients that gradually transition acoustic impedance from water to tissue, reducing reflection. The lipid composition varies with depth, creating a graded impedance profile.

Key Parameter

The loss modulus and its frequency dependence, controlled by lipid composition and collagen density.
