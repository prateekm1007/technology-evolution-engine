Title: Cobalt phosphate catalyst for photoelectrochemical water splitting

We report a cobalt phosphate catalyst electrodeposited on titanium dioxide nanotubes that achieves solar-to-hydrogen efficiency of 2 percent. The catalyst operates at neutral pH with minimal degradation over 100 hours.

The photocurrent density is:
J_ph = q * Phi * eta

where J_ph is photocurrent, q is electron charge, Phi is photon flux, eta is quantum efficiency.

Assumptions:
- All photogenerated carriers reach the catalyst.
- The electrolyte is well-mixed.
- The back reaction is negligible.

Limitations:
- The efficiency is far below the 10 percent threshold for practical use.
- The catalyst degrades under acidic conditions.
- The titanium dioxide only absorbs UV light.
