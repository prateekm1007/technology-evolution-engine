Title: Metal-organic framework for efficient atmospheric water harvesting

We report a metal-organic framework sorbent that captures water from air at low relative humidity. The sorbent achieves a working capacity of 0.25 liters per kilogram at 20 percent relative humidity.

The water uptake is modeled as:
Q = Q_max * K * P / (1 + K * P)

where Q is uptake, Q_max is maximum capacity, K is equilibrium constant, P is vapor pressure.

Assumptions:
- The sorbent reaches equilibrium with ambient air.
- Heat losses during regeneration are negligible.
- The system operates at atmospheric pressure.

Limitations:
- The regeneration temperature of 90 degrees limits practical applications.
- The sorbent degrades after 500 cycles.
- The manufacturing cost is high relative to conventional desiccants.
